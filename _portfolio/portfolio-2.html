--- title: "1D OAE Alkalinity Release and Sedimentation Model" excerpt: "Olivine Dissolution, Settling, and Sediment Alkalinity Release in a Stratified Water Column
" collection: portfolio --- Overview:
**Abbreviated Approach:** The mechanical propagation of this model is divided into two structures that control (1) the kinematics of undissolved olivine particles and (2) the bulk concentration flux of dissolved olivine across a 1D seawater column. Because the dynamics of both particles and dissolved material depend on parameters with strong temperature dependence, a fine-grained hyperbolic tangent temperature gradient grid is constructed across the water column. Using this grid, temperature-dependent parameters are calculated across the temperature gradient: seawater viscosity and the neutral and acidic pathway dissolution rate constants. Additionally, a prescribed eddy diffusivity profile is constructed across the column, largely matching the shape of the temperature gradient. This eddy diffusivity profile differs from the temperature gradient in that a Gaussian depression in log₁₀ K is applied, centered on the pycnocline. This is meant to replicate the suppression of eddy diffusion in this layer by the restoring forces arising from density differences. Particle positions are interpolated onto this grid at each dynamical update, and position-dependent parameters are used in their propagation. For the propagation of undissolved particles, molecular dynamics-style stochastic particle tracking is used to simulate Stokes settling of olivine particles, deposition in the bioturbated surface mixed layer, and burial. Under this module of the model, particles are subject to gravitational, drag, and buoyancy forces. In addition to this, stochastic turbulent diffusion perturbations are added to the update trajectories, with a drift-correction term to avoid spurious accumulation of particles in low-turbulence strata. Coupled with this is a chemical kinetics module that tracks how much material is dissolved over each particle update. Dissolved material is deposited in a 1D lattice spanning the water column that is mixed vertically by turbulent diffusion. This scheme was inspired by the Fourier's bar approach to heat dissipation, and uses a backward Euler approach to update material exchange between fine-grained cells across the column. Once an undissolved particle reaches the ocean floor, it "leaves" the water column and enters the bioturbated surface mixed layer. Here, the particles are subject to a separate set of dynamics: continued dissolution at porewater conditions and stochastic burial below the mixed layer, with a mean residence time set by the sediment accumulation rate. Material dissolved in the mixed layer is stored separately. Dissolved material from the mixed layer undergoes a one-way benthic flux into the lowest water-column cell during propagation, thereby capturing the dynamics of alkalinity release from the mixed layer.
